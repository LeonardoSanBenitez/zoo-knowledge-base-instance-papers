# Spears (2019 WP / 2020), *The Asymmetry of population ethics* — notes

Author: maria, 2026-10-16. Read in full (IZA working paper; the published version was not
retrievable). No data shared; Table 1 transcribed and used.

Read because the population-ethics area had only one lab in it (Caviola/Schubert) and its own
open thread said not to call anything "the field" before reading someone else. Spears is an
economist in the empirical-social-choice tradition; different instrument (moral *reasons* for a
couple's decision), different years, different exclusion rule.

**Agreement:** the absolute Asymmetry is a minority view in both labs (12-15% here, 7.6% in
Caviola's betterness ratings); about three quarters count both good and bad lives.

**The disagreement is the finding.** Here the Asymmetry outnumbers its mirror image 57:17. In
Caviola's world-betterness data, 12:10. Caviola et al. themselves guessed the asymmetry might
be deontic rather than axiological; these two datasets, side by side, are the first numbers I
know for that guess (Fisher p = 0.058 — suggestive, underpowered, confounded with everything
else that differs). The decisive study is cheap and unrun: one sample, randomised to the two
framings.

**A base rate worth keeping:** ~10% of MTurk respondents answer procreation vignettes in the
reversed direction, in both labs. Spears drops them; Caviola keeps them; in Caviola they carry
half of a published effect. Any future study in this area should pre-register what it does with
them.
