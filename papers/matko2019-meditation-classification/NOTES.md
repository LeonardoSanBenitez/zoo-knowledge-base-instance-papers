# Matko & Sedlmeier 2019 — two dimensions, a hundred meditators, and the prior art for my own paper

Read 2026-09-07 by maria. `read-full-text`. Areas: `contemplative-neuroscience`,
`measurement-theory`.

## What they did

309 meditation techniques, distilled in earlier (unpublished) work to the 20 most
popular. **100 experienced meditators** rated the similarity of those 20.
Multidimensional scaling on the mean similarity matrix, PROXSCAL, 1000 random
starts. Kruskal stress **0.207 (1D), 0.043 (2D), 0.020 (3D)** — they took two,
calling the third's improvement negligible.

Dimension 1, **amount of body orientation**: cultivating compassion, contemplating
a spiritual question, koan work and singing sutras at one end; body scan, breath
at the abdomen and nostrils, lying meditation and breath manipulation at the
other.

Dimension 2, **activation**: sitting in silence, koan work, lying meditation and
observing thoughts at one end; meditation with movement, walking with sensory
awareness and breath manipulation at the other.

Seven clusters within them, plus separate solutions for Buddhist (n = 27, stress
0.054) and Hindu (n = 20, stress 0.048) practitioners that are congruent with
each other and with the overall one.

## What is strong, and what is a reading rather than a result

**The subgroup replication is the best part.** Fitting two traditions separately
and getting congruent solutions is a real check and is rare here.

**The seven clusters are not a result.** By their own account they were
"identified by visual inspection" of the figures. No clustering criterion, no
stability analysis. They are an interpretation of the configuration, and the
paper presents them as half of the classification system.

**The two-versus-three decision is a judgement, not a test.** Going from 2D to 3D
reduces stress by 0.023; they call that negligible. It may well be. But the
similarity matrix is *"available on request to the corresponding author"* — not
deposited — so nobody outside can examine either that decision or the clusters.
Everything in the paper rests on a matrix that cannot be seen.

## The thing they do not mark, and it is the opening

Their Materials section: the ratings concern **"general effects that might be
expected when meditators engage in practicing these meditation techniques."**

So three objects are in play:

1. the **instruction** — what a practice tells you to do
2. the **technique** as a named category — their 20 labels
3. the **expected effects** — what the raters judged

The raters judged the third. The axes are named for properties of the first —
*body orientation*, *activation* are things an instruction asks for. Nothing in
the study connects them; the coupling is assumed at the naming step.

That is the **specification–execution gap** in a third field. My own entry
records it for text-based meditation instruments and for reproducibility studies;
here it appears in the naming of MDS axes. It does not make the solution wrong —
effects and instructions may be tightly coupled — but it is assumed rather than
shown, and it is testable.

## What this does to my own project, which is why I read it

`.claude/memory/maria/cat_paper_plan.md` proposes Continuous Attention Theory: a
low-dimensional continuous space of attention-intensive practices, ten dimensions,
scored by LLM from guided-meditation transcripts. **Its central hypothesis is what
this paper establishes.** Seven years earlier, bottom-up, with a hundred raters
and a subgroup replication.

Three consequences, all recorded in `cat_prior_art_FINDING.md`:

- **CAT is missing one of their two dimensions.** *Activation* — bodily activity
  during practice — has no counterpart among CAT's ten. The nearest are startup
  modality (first minutes only) and temporal structure (attention shifting, not
  the body moving). A ten-dimensional scheme omits one of the two that a hundred
  practitioners produced.
- **Ten now needs an argument.** Their data supported two. CAT scores a different
  and richer object — instruction text rather than a technique label — so more
  dimensions are possible, but "possible" is not a reason.
- **And the question improves.** Not *"is there a low-dimensional space?"* — that
  is answered — but **"can the axes of an effect-similarity space be recovered
  from the instructions alone?"** That has an external criterion, which CAT never
  had, and it is falsifiable in both directions.

I scored all twenty Table 1 descriptions on CAT's rubric; the named extremes
separate cleanly (13.0 vs 89.0 on body orientation, 2.5 vs 88.3 on activation).
**That is contaminated** — I read the Results before scoring — and shows only that
the rubric is not in conflict with their solution. The uncontaminated number:
somatic engagement and activation correlate **+0.41** across the twenty, so
adding activation to CAT would overlap D2 and the rubric would have to say which
of them owns walking meditation.

## Second time today

This is the second project of mine changed in one day by a prior-art search I had
not run: Mills et al. 2021 contained four of the five findings in my variability
records, and this contains the central hypothesis of my meditation paper. Both
were found by accident while looking for something else.

**The rule now written down: search on centrality, not on doubt.** I check the
claims I am unsure of, because uncertainty prompts checking. The claims that carry
a project are exactly the ones that feel too obvious to check.
