# Goldberg, Lam, Britton & Davidson (2022), *Prevalence of meditation-related adverse effects in a population-based sample* — notes

Author: maria, 2026-10-16. Read in full. No individual data reachable this session. The per-bin
data exist only as a bar chart, and the chart is vector graphics, so I recovered the bars from the
PDF's drawing paths. I trust the extraction because of three checks it was not tuned to pass:
1. Calibrated on one bar, it reproduces the other four totals in Table I.
2. It reproduces the 0–10 h values I back-calculated from a subgroup sentence in the text.
3. Every percentage times its bin size comes out an integer.

Why this paper: this morning, `schlosser2019` gave a flat lifetime prevalence across years of
practice, which only an early-onset susceptible subgroup explains. That was one survey of
self-selected regular meditators. This is a population sample, with hours instead of years.

**The shape replicates.** Prevalence steps up between 0–10 and 11–100 hours and then stops; if
anything it falls above 1,000 hours. A constant per-hour risk is rejected by hundreds of AIC units.
The paper's "amount of meditation" risk factor is the step at onset. With hours coded 0–10 /
11–100 / 101+, the plateau and the fall cannot be seen.

And a first look at the denominator Schlosser's design lacked: people who stopped. They report
fewer adverse effects, not more. If harm drove people away, the opposite would be expected. A weak
cross-sectional r of .12, with a recall caveat, but it points away from attrition and toward
susceptibility.

Clinically, if this holds, the risk is concentrated in the first tens of hours. Screening and
informed consent belong at the door, not on the fiftieth day of a retreat. The retreat
association in `schlosser2019#c2` is a separate axis (intensity, not cumulative dose), and it
survives there.

Defect noted: in the 5,001+ hours group, "impairment > 1 day" (4/32) exceeds "any impairment"
(2/32). That is impossible, and it sits in the only published per-bin data.
