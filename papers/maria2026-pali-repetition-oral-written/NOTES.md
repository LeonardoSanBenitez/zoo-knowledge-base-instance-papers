## The numbers

Share of tokens inside a verbatim word 8-gram that recurs in the same 20,000-token window:

| text | R8 |
|---|---|
| suttas, DN / MN (mula) | **0.59 / 0.59** |
| commentaries (atthakatha) | 0.02 / 0.02 |
| sub-commentaries (tika) | 0.003 / 0.003 |
| Abhidhamma (Dhammasangani + Vibhanga) | **0.67** |
| Visuddhimagga | 0.06 |

Paired sutta by sutta against its own commentary, at equal length, the canon repeats more in 44 of 44
pairs. DN 25 alone gives 0.53, between Allon's 30% verbatim and his 65% "involving repetition", which is
what an exact 8-gram measure should give. Shuffling the words inside any window drives every figure to
0.000. The edition elides repetitions as "...pe...", and elides the canon most (5 per 1,000 tokens in DN,
29 in the Abhidhamma). So every canonical figure here is a floor.

## What it means, and what it does not

The obvious objection to "repetition is oral residue" is genre. Discourses repeat; exegesis does not.
The Abhidhamma answers part of it. It is not discourse. It is scholastic analysis, the most list-bound
text in the canon, and it repeats more than the suttas. The Visuddhimagga is scholastic and list-bound
too, but composed in writing, and it barely repeats. So within this edition, heavy verbatim repetition
travels with canonical transmission, across genres, and its absence travels with written composition.

That is what the oral-transmission reading predicts. It is not proof of it. The Abhidhamma's
repetitions come from its matika method of running every term through every classification. One can
call that mnemonic engineering or merely combinatorics, and the counts cannot choose. There is one
text per category. And the commentaries' Sinhala forerunners may themselves have been oral, so their
Pali form tests a written redaction, not an origin.

## Status or composition (addendum 2, pre-registered)

Three more Abhidhamma books are as repetitive as the suttas or more (0.56-0.89). Three late books that the
Burmese canon took in, but that were composed as books, are not. The Milindapanha is at 0.26, the
Nettippakarana at 0.21, the Petakopadesa at 0.03. I predicted all three below 0.10, and that failed.
They are not near the canon either. So it is a gradient, not a switch: the canon proper, then books
composed in the canonical milieu, then the written commentaries and treatise. Composition explains most
of it. The milieu explains the rest. I wrote the prediction too cleanly, and the failure is the more
interesting result.

## For myself

I have sat with these repetitions in the sutta readings at the sangha, where they are recited and
nobody finds them strange. To a reader on a page they are tedious. To a chanting voice they are a
road. The numbers say only that the road was built where the voice was, and not where the pen was.
