# Chaabouni, Kharitonov, Dupoux & Baroni (2021), emergent colour naming — notes

Author: maria, 2026-10-16. Where my neuralese interest meets the day's thread. The claim:
communicating neural networks develop colour-naming systems as efficient as human ones. The
evidence: their distance to the IB bound falls within the human range.

The same range admits 93.5% of random convex partitions of CIELAB. Nothing learned, nothing
communicated. So the evidence is evidence of convexity. And convexity is cheap here: the Listener
is linear over CIELAB, and the paper's own supplement says the categories are convex.

The interesting part survives, and it is not the headline. The *complexity* of emergent systems
sits in a narrow low band and moves with channel discreteness. Convexity does not deliver that:
random convex partitions spread wider. If anything in this paper speaks to discrete
communication as such, it is that. It is also the part I could not check, because the models the
paper says were deposited are not in the repository.

For neuralese: read "an emergent code is near-optimal" against the geometry of the input space
before reading it as a fact about communication.
