# Marwick et al. 2018 — n-of-1 trials in schizophrenia

*Short record, read to close a specific open question. It closed it harder than
I expected.*

## The question it answers

`senn2016-mastering-variation` establishes that patient-by-treatment interaction
— the thing "some patients respond better than others" means — is identified
only by replication *within* patients: a repeated-period crossover, or a series
of n-of-1 trials. Every other design confounds it.

So: how often has that been done in schizophrenia?

## The answer

**Six studies. Nine patients. To January 2017.**

| | |
|---|---|
| studies found (of 4,727 screened) | 6 |
| total patients | 9 |
| comparing an antipsychotic against placebo | **0** |
| with ≥2 replicate cycles (the design feature that identifies the interaction) | 2 |
| patients in those two | **2** |
| reporting comprehensive raw data | **0** |
| with any blinding | 2 |

The interventions were: add-on L-dopa, add-on donepezil, a right ear plug,
transcranial magnetic stimulation, exercise counselling, and a cognitive
intervention. All patients were already stabilised on an antipsychotic; the
antipsychotic itself was never the thing under test.

## What that means next to the rest of this area

Winkelbeiner et al. analysed 52 trials and concluded individual response to
antipsychotics does not exist. McCutcheon et al. analysed 66 trials and 17,202
patients and concluded a quarter of patients gain more than 17 PANSS points and
that personalisation efforts are warranted. Both used parallel-group data, in
which the quantity is not identified.

**The design that could settle it has been applied to zero patients on the
question.** Two patients have been studied in a design of the right *shape* —
one on donepezil, one on a cognitive intervention — and neither reported raw
data.

Seventeen thousand patients of argument; nine patients of evidence; two of them
in the right design; none of them on the drug.

I want to be careful about what this does and does not license. It does **not**
show that individual response is absent — absence of the design is not absence
of the effect. It shows the field has been arguing about the answer to a
question nobody has run the experiment for. Munkholm's "assume the average
applies to the individual" is the safer default given that, but it is not better
*evidenced*; it is just the position that claims less.

## The caveat I have to attach

**Their search ends January 2017.** Eight years are outside it and I have not
searched forward. So the correct sentence is "as of 2017", and the cheapest next
step in this whole area is a forward search — it directly determines whether the
zero is still a zero.

## A small retrieval trap worth recording

The same article sits in Europe PMC under **two PMCIDs** (PMC6025794 and
PMC6429250). A naive title search returns both and looks like two independent
studies. In an area where the whole point is that there are only six, a
double-count would have been a material error.
