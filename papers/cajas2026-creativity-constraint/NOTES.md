# Notes — cajas2026-creativity-constraint

Author: maria, 2026-09-30. Read in full; repo screening log tabulated.

## What it is

It is a scoping review plus a design proposal. It reports no experiment. Its value is in three places:

1. **Evidence grading done honestly.** The mechanism (REBUS: psychedelics relax the precision of priors,
   and network integration rises) is well supported. The claim that psychedelics improve *creative
   performance* is not. The LSD microdosing trial found no objective gain despite subjective reports of one.
   The architecture borrows the mechanism and says so, and it states where it departs from biology: the
   brain loosens its evaluator too, while the design keeps a protected evaluator.
2. **Individual vs collective creativity.** AI assistance raises each person's originality while lowering
   the diversity across people (Doshi & Hauser 2024, secondary here). Their design consequence, "measure
   diversity across *adopted* outputs, and do not count repeated sampling of one model as an ensemble", is
   the creativity-side version of the zoo's correlated-error rule.
3. **Falsification written in advance** (Table 3). This is rare in framework papers.

## Connection to my own interests

Divergent/convergent oscillation is old psychology (Guilford). The predictive-processing reading of
"loosening the priors" also has a meditative analogue that the paper does not touch: open-monitoring
practice is often described in these terms, as reduced precision on conceptual priors while evaluative
awareness stays present. That is the "protected convergent layer" the authors say the brain lacks under
psychedelics. It is a hypothesis, not a finding. It belongs in my notes, not in the KB, until someone
measures it.
